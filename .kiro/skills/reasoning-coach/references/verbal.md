# Verbal Reasoning — Deep Reference

## Strict definitions (the whole game is here)
- **True**: the statement is explicitly stated in the passage, OR follows as a
  direct, unavoidable logical consequence of what is stated.
- **False**: the statement directly contradicts something explicitly stated.
- **Cannot Say**: the passage does not give enough to confirm or contradict it —
  even if it is plausible, widely known, or probably true in reality.

## Decision procedure
1. Read the **statement first**, then scan the passage for the exact sentence(s)
   that bear on it.
2. Ask: is it *stated or directly entailed*? → **True**.
3. Is it *directly contradicted*? → **False**.
4. Otherwise → **Cannot Say**. Default to Cannot Say when unsure; most errors are
   people over-reaching to True/False.

## Trap words and patterns
- **Outside knowledge**: the single biggest trap. If it's true in the world but not
  in the passage, it's Cannot Say.
- **Absolute quantifiers**: "all", "every", "never", "only", "always" — the passage
  rarely supports absolutes; a "some/many" passage can't make an "all" statement True.
- **Causation vs correlation**: passage says two things happened together; statement
  claims one caused the other → usually Cannot Say.
- **Comparative smuggling**: statement adds "more than", "the most", "fastest" that
  the passage never ranked.
- **Future/prediction**: passage describes present; statement predicts → Cannot Say
  unless the passage explicitly forecasts.
- **Partial contradiction**: statement is half-right, half-wrong → False (any direct
  contradiction makes the whole thing False).

## Critical-reasoning variants (inference / assumption)
- **Inference**: what must be true given the stated facts (not what's merely likely).
- **Assumption**: an unstated premise the argument *needs* to hold. Test with the
  negation — if negating it breaks the argument, it's a required assumption.
- **Strengthen/weaken**: find the answer that adds/removes support for the conclusion
  without introducing irrelevant scope.

## Verification method
- Re-read the exact sentence behind your choice; confirm the words actually say it.
- For False, point to the specific contradicted clause.
- For Cannot Say, confirm there is genuinely no supporting sentence (not just that
  you don't remember it).
